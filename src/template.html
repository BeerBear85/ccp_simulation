The start jerk is set by momentum: the rider must go from 0 to cable speed (Δp ≈ m·Δv). Sliding: low carpet friction, but the full Δv. Jump: take-off just before the pull, so Δv is ≈2.6 m/s smaller. In both the arms and body yield above ≈0.95 kN, and the cable deflects under the pull.

How to ride

Hold ← → (or the buttons in the 3D view) to lean: a tap gives a small edge (10°), and the lean builds up the longer you hold, to the maximum 45° after ≈1.2 s (the bar under the button shows how far). Let go to ride flat again. Lean out (right) before the corners to keep the line tight and avoid a jerk. Lean in (left) to reach the obstacles, but ride flat (lean ≈ 0°) up kickers and land flat: an edge on landing or a hit corner means a fall. Hold ↓ (Crouch) to absorb onto a box or on landing, and press ↑ (Pop) just before the kicker lip for a bigger jump. If your knees bottom out on a hard landing, you fall. Space pauses, . steps once.

Tricks. Hold Q (left) or R (right) on the kicker before the lip to pre-wind: the twist against the ramp starts the spin (≈0.4 s for a full wind-up). In the air, keep holding to add a little rotation through the line; the spin is otherwise fixed by angular momentum, and a grab tucks you and speeds it up. Hold W for a nose grab or S for a tail grab (in the air only; one hand leaves the handle, so the release limit drops to 60 %). Let go of the grab before touchdown and land within 40° of straight or switch; a 180 lands you switch. The buttons above Crouch/Pop do the same on touch screens.

Rails and boxes. Off the water there is no side force from the board; only the contact, rail friction and the line act. The line locks your speed component along the line, so a rail pointing away from the cable needs v_req = (l̂·v_c)/(l̂·t), often well above cable speed: build speed with an edge before the feature. Too slow and the line tightens and pulls you sideways towards the cable; faster than v_req and the line goes slack. The rider balances automatically over the contact patch (your lean adds 30 %). Hold Q/R on a rail for a boardslide: the board across the rail tolerates ±0.7 m of side drift instead of ±0.2 m, but turn back before the end or you land sideways. Approach with a little sideways speed away from the cable so the side pull has the whole width to slow it.

Physics from the report · validation

The model has been checked against the report section by section (1–19 and appendices). It follows "Physics and Simulation of a Cable-Park Wakeboard Rider" and sits between levels M2 and M3: rider and board with translation, yaw, roll, a reduced-coordinate multi-body rider (knees as two-link legs with torque limits, fore–aft body lean with ankle and hip balance strategies, arms that raise/lower and yield the handle, pose-dependent de Leva segment inertia), a unilateral line, one water-model function throughout, and a moving water surface (wind chop + own wake).
